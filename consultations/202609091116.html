1. Purpose of Assessment

This assessment was performed to document the present condition of a mature red maple, evaluate recent canopy discoloration and associated landscape stress, identify likely contributing factors, and develop a practical preservation and landscape rehabilitation plan.

This report is intended primarily as a tree-care and land-management assessment. It is not presented as a forensic determination of liability or as a definitive diagnosis of a single causal agent.

2. Tree Description

The subject tree is a mature red maple (Acer rubrum) estimated at approximately 25 inches diameter at breast height (DBH), approximately 50 feet in height, and approximately 50 feet in crown spread. The tree is estimated to be approximately 40–50 years old and has codominant stem architecture.

The root flare is visible and planting depth appears appropriate. No significant stem-girdling roots were observed during the current visual assessment. No notable mower or string-trimmer injury was observed at the base, and no obvious evidence of active borer infestation was identified. Minor trunk cavitation and some evidence consistent with sunscald were observed.

3. Documented Site History

2025 Growing Season The maple was observed to be green, dense, and visually vigorous. No significant premature coloration, thinning, canopy decline, or comparable late-summer browning was noted.
Spring 2025 Structural pruning was performed primarily on the east-to-northeast portion of the canopy. The tree responded with strong, dark-green foliage and maintained a healthy appearance for more than one year following the pruning.
Fall 2025 Major utility excavation occurred within the established root zone. Photographic records document a trench running generally south to north along the southwest-to-west side of the tree.
Spring 2026 The maple reportedly leafed out normally.
Late July 2026 Premature foliar discoloration was first noted.
Late Summer to September 2026 Browning progressively expanded through portions of the crown. Brown foliage remained largely retained. No major portion of the tree has yet been confirmed dead.

4. Utility Excavation Within Root Zone

Historical photographs document substantial soil disturbance within the root zone during fall 2025. The trench appears to have been approximately 18 inches wide and passed approximately 10–15 feet from the trunk on the southwest-to-west side. Exact trench depth and utility type are presently unknown.

Root severance was not directly observed or documented at the time of excavation. However, given the size and maturity of the tree and the proximity of the excavation, disturbance and/or severance of established roots within the affected area is considered probable.

5. Present Crown Condition

The tree presently exhibits extensive premature browning and retained foliage throughout a substantial portion of the canopy. Symptoms are most pronounced on the southwest side of the crown. The east-to-northeast portion remains comparatively greener and visually more vigorous.

The asymmetric pattern is significant because the area of greatest canopy stress generally corresponds with the side of the root zone subjected to the documented 2025 excavation.

Primary Current Finding Severe asymmetric crown stress is present, with the greatest premature browning occurring on the southwest side of the canopy. The spatial and temporal relationship between this symptom pattern and prior root-zone excavation warrants consideration of excavation-related root impairment as a significant contributing factor.

6. Soil and Root-Zone Observations

Surface soil beneath the mulch layer is dark and contains visible organic matter and fine roots. Beneath the amended surface layer, the underlying native soil is red clay.

The visible root flare and appropriate planting depth reduce concern for deep planting as the primary explanation for the present decline. No significant stem-girdling roots were observed during the visual inspection.

7. Irrigation and Moisture Stress

The landscape irrigation system is currently scheduled to operate three times per week for approximately seven minutes per cycle. Irrigation extends beyond the raised planting bed and into the surrounding lawn.

Controller run time alone does not establish the actual amount or uniformity of water delivered to the root zone. Soil moisture availability, sprinkler precipitation rate, distribution uniformity, infiltration into clay soil, and seasonal evapotranspirational demand should therefore be evaluated before increasing or substantially modifying irrigation.

Several shallow-rooted ornamental plants are also showing stress despite not occupying the excavated portion of the root zone. This suggests that moisture stress or irrigation limitations may be contributing to the broader landscape condition independently of the trenching event.

8. Surrounding Landscape Condition

The surrounding planting bed contains both viable and stressed vegetation. Several shallow-rooted shrubs and ornamental plants show visible decline or reduced vigor. Other drought-tolerant plants remain functional and flowering.

The broader pattern supports a site-level assessment rather than evaluation of the maple in isolation. Water availability, competition, heat exposure, soil characteristics, root disturbance, and irrigation performance may be interacting.

9. Assessment

The subject red maple was apparently vigorous through the 2025 growing season and showed no comparable premature discoloration, thinning, or decline prior to the documented utility excavation.

Significant soil disturbance subsequently occurred within the established root zone during fall 2025. The tree leafed out normally the following spring, with premature discoloration first becoming apparent in late July 2026 and progressing through the summer.

The temporal sequence and the spatial relationship between the disturbed root zone and the most heavily affected portion of the canopy make excavation-related root impairment a significant suspected contributor to the present stress.

Concurrent moisture limitation may also be contributing. The existing irrigation schedule may or may not provide sufficient water to the effective root zone, and stress observed in shallow-rooted ornamental vegetation outside the excavated area suggests that broader site moisture conditions should be evaluated.

At present, available evidence does not establish a single definitive causal mechanism. The most reasonable working assessment is that the tree is experiencing substantial physiological stress potentially associated with a combination of root-zone disturbance and summer water limitation.

11. Recommendations

Immediate

  • Preserve the existing tree and avoid additional excavation, compaction, grade change, or root disturbance within the root zone.
  • Perform an irrigation audit to determine actual precipitation rate and distribution around the maple rather than relying only on controller run time.
  • Compare soil moisture on the southwest and northeast portions of the root zone at multiple distances from the trunk.
  • Maintain organic mulch over appropriate portions of the root zone while keeping mulch away from direct contact with the trunk.
  • Avoid unnecessary fertilization while the tree is under active stress.

Landscape Rehabilitation

  • Remove nonviable ornamental plants.
  • Selectively prune clearly dead or nonrecoverable ornamental growth while retaining viable plant material.
  • Evaluate lawn mowing height and maintain turf at a height appropriate for summer heat stress, generally avoiding close mowing.
  • Evaluate irrigation coverage throughout both the lawn and planting bed.
  • Consider replacement plantings selected for the site's heat, soil, light, and water conditions.

Tree Monitoring

  • Monitor retained brown foliage and branch vitality through dormancy.
  • Reassess twig and branch viability after leaf drop.
  • Reinspect the tree during spring 2027 leaf-out.
  • Document whether previously browned crown sectors leaf out normally, weakly, partially, or not at all.
  • If decline progresses despite correction of site moisture conditions, consider additional diagnostic investigation for vascular disease or other internal causes.

12. Prognosis

Preservation remains reasonable at this stage because no major portion of the crown has yet been confirmed dead and the tree retains a substantial area of green foliage.

Prognosis should remain guarded until the tree completes dormancy and its spring 2027 response can be evaluated. The extent of any root loss from the prior excavation is presently unknown, and recovery will depend upon the amount of remaining functional root system, summer moisture availability, and the tree's ability to regenerate absorbing roots.

Management Objective Reduce additional physiological stress, preserve the remaining root system, improve effective water availability where indicated, rehabilitate the surrounding landscape, and evaluate the tree's recovery response over the next growing cycle.

13. Limitations

This assessment is based on visual inspection, available site history, photographs, client-reported information, and current field observations. No laboratory testing, root excavation, aerial inspection, resistance drilling, tomography, or subsurface root mapping was performed unless otherwise noted.

The exact depth of the prior utility trench, the utility involved, and the number, diameter, or condition of roots affected by the excavation are presently unknown. Root severance is therefore inferred from the location and nature of the disturbance rather than directly documented.

Tree condition can change over time, particularly following root injury, drought, storms, disease, or other environmental stress. Continued monitoring is recommended.

14. Recommended Additional Documentation

  • Photograph the tree from north, east, south, and west.
  • Photograph the root flare from all four cardinal directions.
  • Measure exact DBH.
  • Measure exact trunk-to-former-trench distance.
  • Photograph the current site from approximately the same position as the fall 2025 trenching photographs.

  • Record comparative soil moisture readings across the root zone.
