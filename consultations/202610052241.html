Scope, terminology & limitations

Purpose and evidence

This report organizes nine notable-tree records and a general boundary-management observation for the Keenan property. Observations and original risk impressions are taken from Christian Castillo’s supplied field notes, dated by specimen IDs from September 30 through October 5, 2026. It is not a complete inventory of every tree on or near the property. Approximate distances and species identifications are retained from those notes.

Inspection methods to complete before issue

[Record actual viewing method, accessible areas, tools used, weather and visibility, and any measurements, sounding, probing, aerial inspection, or advanced testing performed.] Photographic placeholders are a shot list, not evidence that those views or examinations have already been obtained.

Risk terminology

“High,” “moderate,” and other risk descriptions reproduce preliminary field-note impressions. The notes do not provide a defined assessment time frame, likelihood-of-failure and impact evaluations, or a complete consequences analysis. Accordingly, these descriptions are not represented as formal ISA matrix ratings. Action priority and tree vitality are separate from a formal risk rating.

Pruning objectives

Selective reduction shortens identified branches to suitable lateral branches to reduce leverage or achieve clearance. Final prescriptions should identify branches, desired clearance, and appropriate cut limits. Avoid topping and excessive live-crown removal. Pruning does not repair decayed basal wood or ensure that a tree will fail away from a structure.

Ownership and changing conditions

Greenbelt and homeowner locations are reported observations, not surveyed ownership determinations. Confirm responsibility and permission before work. No visible decay does not rule out hidden defects. Tree conditions can change; this review does not guarantee against failure. New cracking, stem separation, root-plate movement, or newly hanging limbs warrant prompt reassessment.

Reference and contact

Pruning terminology reference: International Society of Arboriculture — Pruning Mature Trees. Reference supports general practice, not the individual tree findings.
