02 / Horticulture

Evergreen privacy restoration

To restore some of the privacy lost following the removal of eastern red cedar, we propose establishing evergreen screening along selected portions of the fence line.

Recommended cultivar

Nellie R. Stevens Holly

Nellie R. Stevens holly is a vigorous, broadleaf evergreen with dense, glossy, dark-green foliage and a naturally upright, pyramidal growth habit.

Its year-round foliage makes it particularly well suited to the development of a living privacy screen while adding diversity to the existing woodland landscape.

Recommended planting window

For container-grown hollies in central Oklahoma, our preferred planting period extends from late autumn through early spring.

October, November, and March offer particularly favorable planting opportunities, while December, January, February, and April may also provide suitable conditions when weather and soil moisture are favorable.

Advantages of cooler-season planting

Cooler temperatures generally reduce transpiration and irrigation demand, easing the transition from nursery containers into the surrounding soil.

When soil temperatures remain favorable, newly installed hollies can begin establishing their root systems before encountering the environmental demands of an Oklahoma summer.

Given the potential for early and rapidly developing spring heat in central Oklahoma, establishing these hollies during favorable late-autumn or winter conditions may be advantageous.

Although early spring is generally favored for broadleaf evergreen establishment, winter planting remains a viable option when soils are workable, moisture is adequate, and severe freezing conditions or desiccating winds are not anticipated.

Establishment care

Newly planted hollies will require consistent soil moisture, appropriate mulching, and periodic watering throughout their establishment period, including during dry winter weather.

The calendar represents approximate seasonal suitability rather than fixed planting dates. Final installation timing will be determined by prevailing weather, soil conditions, nursery availability, and irrigation accessibility.

03 / Seasonal Maintenance

Fall landscape cleanup

Seasonal cleanup of selected portions of the property, addressing accumulated leaves, loose organic debris, and general landscape tidying through a gentle, low-impact approach.

Work will be prioritized according to the areas of greatest concern while retaining beneficial organic material in appropriate woodland areas.

Protecting the landscape from the ground up

Our approach emphasizes manual leaf collection and lightweight equipment rather than bringing heavy machinery across the landscape and through the root zones of established trees.

Much of a tree's fine absorbing root system occupies the upper layers of the soil, where it is particularly susceptible to compaction from repeated equipment traffic.

By minimizing heavy equipment and tire traffic, we can reduce unnecessary disturbance to the soil while accomplishing the desired aesthetic improvements.

Benefits of low-impact cleanup

  • Reduced soil compaction. Limiting heavy equipment traffic helps preserve the root zones of established trees, particularly when soils are moist.
  • Preservation of soil structure. Maintaining soil porosity supports oxygen exchange, water infiltration, and healthy root function.
  • Reduced landscape disturbance. Manual collection minimizes the potential for tire rutting, turf damage, and injury to shallow roots.
  • Greater precision. Gentle cleanup allows for more selective work around existing plants, tree trunks, and landscape features.
  • Retention of beneficial organic matter. Appropriate woodland areas may retain leaf litter to support nutrient cycling, soil biology, and moisture conservation.

The objective is not simply to remove leaves, but to improve the appearance of the property without compromising the health of the landscape beneath them.
