Field Operations Development

Build skill. Earn trust. Increase your value.

This career ladder explains how crew members can advance within Abundant Life Land & Tree. Pay increases as a team member demonstrates greater safety, reliability, technical ability, judgment, productivity, and independence.

View the career ladder Review the skills checklist

What advancement means here

Advancement is not based only on seniority or physical effort. It is based on demonstrated capability and the value a crew member creates for the company, the client, and the rest of the team.

  • Work safely without repeated correction
  • Produce quality work at an efficient pace
  • Care for equipment and client property
  • Recognize problems before they become costly
  • Require less supervision over time

How pay is determined

Four principles guide compensation

These standards are intended to make advancement understandable, consistent, and fair.

1

Safety comes first

No amount of speed or physical strength compensates for unsafe conduct, ignored hazards, or careless equipment use.

2

Reliability creates value

Attendance, punctuality, preparation, communication, and the ability to complete scheduled work all affect compensation.

3

Skill increases earning power

Technical knowledge, equipment competency, judgment, and problem-solving allow the company to complete more valuable work.

4

Independence earns more

A crew member becomes more valuable as they can be trusted to make sound decisions and produce results with less supervision.

Field operations career ladder

A clear path from crew support to independent production

The listed pay ranges are guidelines. Placement within a range depends on demonstrated skill, reliability, productivity, safety, and current business needs.

1

Crew Laborer

Build dependable work habits and basic field competency.

$16–$18/hr

Core responsibilities

  • Arrive on time and prepared for weather and workload
  • Wear required PPE without repeated reminders
  • Drag brush and stage debris efficiently
  • Move manageable wood using safe lifting practices
  • Perform raking, blowing, and final cleanup
  • Protect lawns, structures, vehicles, and landscaping

Required behaviors

  • Follow instructions accurately
  • Stay engaged without excessive idle time
  • Communicate fatigue, heat stress, and hazards promptly
  • Ask questions when instructions are unclear
  • Maintain a respectful and professional attitude
Supervision level

Works under direct supervision and receives frequent task assignments throughout the day.

2

Ground Technician I

Perform routine ground operations with growing consistency.

$18–$21/hr

Technical skills

  • Operate blowers, trimmers, and basic powered equipment
  • Fuel and oil equipment correctly
  • Inspect tools before and after use
  • Recognize common jobsite hazards
  • Use basic knots taught by crew leadership
  • Stage tools and materials for efficient workflow

Production expectations

  • Complete routine tasks without constant prompting
  • Maintain a steady pace throughout the work period
  • Notice obvious cleanup and organization needs
  • Handle tools and materials without unnecessary damage
  • Support more experienced crew members effectively
Supervision level

Receives clear assignments and can complete routine tasks with periodic check-ins.

3

Ground Technician II

Become a productive and technically capable ground worker.

$21–$24/hr

Technical skills

  • Operate a chainsaw safely for routine ground cutting
  • Identify kickback zones and dangerous cutting positions
  • Recognize basic tension and compression
  • Change, tension, clean, and inspect chains
  • Perform basic hand filing with inspection afterward
  • Use lowering lines under direct instruction

Production expectations

  • Anticipate common next steps
  • Organize brush and wood for efficient removal
  • Maintain saw readiness during production
  • Communicate clearly with climbers and sawyers
  • Help prevent bottlenecks in cleanup and processing
Supervision level

Handles ordinary ground tasks independently but still requires direction for technical or unusual situations.

4

Skilled Ground Technician

Support advanced production with strong judgment and skill.

$24–$27/hr

Technical skills

  • Run ground saws competently and efficiently
  • Sharpen chains to a reliable working standard
  • Read tension, compression, bind, and likely movement
  • Select the correct saw and cutting approach
  • Understand common rigging terminology and commands
  • Operate ropes smoothly under a climber's direction

Production expectations

  • Keep production moving without repeated instruction
  • Identify dull, damaged, or improperly functioning tools
  • Direct less-experienced crew during routine work
  • Recognize when a plan needs to pause or be reconsidered
  • Maintain quality during physically demanding work
Supervision level

Requires only periodic direction during familiar work and can be trusted with important production responsibilities.

5

Lead Ground Technician High leverage

Increase company capacity by leading ground production.

$27–$31/hr

Leadership skills

  • Read the work order and understand the job objective
  • Set up tools, work zones, and equipment efficiently
  • Direct ground operations while the climber is aloft
  • Make sound routine decisions without owner approval
  • Train newer workers in safe procedures
  • Communicate professionally with clients when needed

Business value

  • Can maintain production when the owner steps away
  • Prevents avoidable damage, downtime, and wasted labor
  • Recognizes equipment and material needs in advance
  • Helps work remain within the planned labor budget
  • Can complete portions of a job independently
Supervision level

Trusted to lead routine ground operations and maintain safe, profitable production without continuous owner presence.

6

Crew Leader

Run ordinary jobs safely, professionally, and profitably.

$31–$36/hr

Job leadership

  • Verify crew, tools, equipment, and job documentation
  • Lead the job briefing and jobsite hazard assessment
  • Assign roles based on experience and current conditions
  • Track progress against scope and planned labor
  • Handle routine client communication and concerns
  • Complete documentation and end-of-job inspection

Decision-making

  • Stop work when conditions become unsafe
  • Escalate scope changes and major hazards to ownership
  • Protect company equipment, reputation, and profitability
  • Resolve ordinary workflow problems independently
  • Hold crew members accountable to company standards
Supervision level

Can lead an ordinary job from arrival through completion with limited owner involvement.

7

Climbing Arborist / Production Lead

Generate revenue through advanced technical production.

$35–$45+/hr

Technical capability

  • Perform appropriate pruning and removal operations
  • Establish and evaluate climbing systems
  • Use rigging systems within demonstrated competency
  • Recognize structural defects and changing hazards
  • Select suitable work methods for tree and site conditions
  • Maintain current knowledge of arboricultural standards

Business capability

  • Lead crews through complex production work
  • Produce high-value work efficiently
  • Identify additional legitimate client needs
  • Explain recommendations professionally
  • Protect the company's reputation and client relationships
Supervision level

Operates as a trusted technical leader capable of producing substantial revenue with broad independence.

Standards that apply at every level

Advancement requires more than technical skill

Attendance and communication

Crew members are expected to report as scheduled, communicate absences as early as reasonably possible, and provide honest information about their readiness to perform assigned work.

Fitness for duty

Crew members must report adequately rested, hydrated, nourished, and physically able to perform assigned work safely. Health concerns and heat-related symptoms must be reported immediately.

Equipment care

Higher compensation requires greater trust. Careless damage, neglected maintenance, lost tools, and misuse of equipment reduce the value a crew member provides.

Client property and professionalism

Every crew member represents the company. Respectful conduct, clean work practices, property protection, and professional communication are required.

Unsafe speed is not productivity

Production is valuable only when it is controlled, repeatable, and safe. Recklessness, unnecessary rushing, skipped PPE, and concealed mistakes are grounds for corrective action.

Pay levels are not automatic

Possessing one advanced skill does not automatically place a crew member at a higher level. Advancement reflects the complete role, including judgment, reliability, safety, and independence.

Promotion process

How to advance to the next level

Promotions are based on demonstrated performance, not promises or time alone. A crew member may ask for a development review when they believe they are consistently performing at the next level.

  • Review the next level's responsibilities
  • Identify skills or behaviors that still need development
  • Demonstrate those abilities consistently on actual jobs
  • Complete a formal or informal competency review
  • Agree on expectations before a pay change takes effect

Evaluation areas

Advancement may consider safety, attendance, physical readiness, technical skill, pace, quality, equipment care, initiative, communication, judgment, leadership, and the ability to work independently.

A temporary strong performance does not necessarily establish a new pay level. The employee should show that the capability is dependable and repeatable.
