The mission

Sales is not about being the cheapest. It is about matching good clients with work we can perform exceptionally well.

A sold job is only a good sale when the customer understands the scope, the production team can deliver what was promised, and the price leaves room to execute safely and profitably.

The standard

Do not create work just to create work.

Create good work: clear scope, appropriate price, realistic expectations, healthy customer relationship, and clean production handoff.

Sales doctrine

Four rules

01

Generate

Conversations create opportunities. Carry cards. Notice properties. Ask questions. Follow relationships.

02

Understand

Diagnose before prescribing. Understand what the client wants, what the property needs, and what problem actually needs solving.

03

Sell value

Price confidently. Explain the work. Do not reduce good tree work to a race toward the lowest bid.

04

Handoff cleanly

What production receives should match what the customer believes they purchased.

Before estimating

Know what you're selling

Customer

Who owns or controls the property? Who makes the final decision? What outcome matters most to them?

Scope

Which trees or areas are included? Removal, pruning, cleanup, hauling, stump work, landscaping, or another service?

Site

Access, gates, fences, structures, utilities, traffic, neighboring property, terrain, drop zones, and equipment limitations.

Timing

Emergency, active hazard, upcoming event, flexible scheduling, seasonal need, or exploratory future work?

Risk

Deadwood, compromised trees, difficult rigging, targets, utility proximity, decay, poor access, or specialized equipment.

Expectations

What does “finished” mean to this client? Make cleanup, haul-off, wood handling, surface protection, and exclusions explicit.

Pricing & presentation

Sell the result, not just the labor

Do not apologize for a good price.

The customer is buying judgment, risk management, skilled labor, equipment, insurance, cleanup, property protection, communication, and a completed result.

Price should support excellent execution. A job that cannot be performed properly at the sold price was not sold well.

When presenting work:

  • Explain what you observed.
  • Explain what you recommend.
  • Explain what the crew will actually do.
  • State the price clearly.
  • Stop talking long enough for the customer to respond.
  • Answer the real concern behind objections.
  • Do not create unnecessary pressure.

Follow-up

Stay useful without becoming noise

Follow-up exists to help the customer make a decision, not to harass them into one.

After estimate

Make sure the proposal arrived and invite questions.

2–3 days

Check for unanswered concerns or changes in scope.

About 1 week

Reconnect politely and ask where the project stands.

Longer-term

Keep worthwhile opportunities alive when timing, budget, or circumstances change.

Production handoff

Never make the crew rediscover the sale.

Before work is scheduled, operations should be able to understand exactly what was sold and what conditions were observed.

Customer Name, address, contact information, preferred communication, decision-maker.
Exact scope Trees, limbs, areas, services, cleanup, haul-off, stump work, exclusions.
Price Contract amount, options accepted, deposits, and approved changes.
Site conditions Access, gates, obstacles, targets, terrain, utilities, neighbors.
Expectations What the customer specifically cares about and what “done” looks like.
Photos / notes Enough information for production to arrive prepared rather than surprised.

Compensation

When sales wins, sales gets paid

20%

Sales commission

20% of gross revenue from salesperson-generated leads that sell, payable upon receipt of customer funds.

The purpose of commission is alignment: creating more valuable work should create more value for both the salesperson and the company.

Lead source and ownership should therefore be recorded clearly when an opportunity enters the pipeline.

Continuous improvement

Build the system around the salesperson

The goal is not merely to have a productive salesman. The goal is to learn how Abundant Life makes salespeople productive.

1

What would help you sell more?

Diagnose the whole system: materials, tools, pricing authority, lead flow, proof of work, process, credibility, or customer experience.

2

What gets in your way of selling?

Find friction: slow estimates, unclear pricing, missing information, weak follow-up, scheduling problems, or unnecessary approvals.

3

What can I do to help you sell more?

Define what the owner can personally do to remove bottlenecks, improve support, and make good opportunities easier to close.

Remember

A sale is a promise.

Sell work we can be proud to perform. Price it so we can perform it well. Describe it so the customer and crew understand the same promise. Then hand it to production cleanly.
