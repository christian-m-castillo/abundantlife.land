This Tree and Landscape Service Agreement (“Agreement”) is between Abundant Life Land & Tree LLC (“Contractor”) and Robert Keenan (“Customer”). Contractor and Customer agree as follows:

Customer represents that Customer owns the service property or has full legal authority from the property owner to authorize the work.

Customer authorizes Contractor and Contractor’s employees, subcontractors, vehicles, trailers, machinery, and equipment to enter the property and use reasonably necessary access routes to perform the work.

Customer represents that all trees, shrubs, planter contents, and other materials identified for removal are located on Customer’s property and may lawfully be removed. Customer is responsible for obtaining any required permission from co-owners, neighbors, homeowners’ associations, landlords, or other interested parties.

Contractor may rely upon Customer’s representations concerning ownership, property lines, and authorization. Customer will be responsible for claims resulting from incorrect boundary information or a lack of authority, except to the extent caused by Contractor’s independently negligent or intentionally wrongful conduct.

2. Scope of work

For the fixed contract price stated below, Contractor will furnish the labor, equipment, and ordinary disposal necessary to perform the following work:

A. Dead oak removal

  1. Remove one medium-to-large dead oak located behind and in close proximity to the residence.
  2. Use climbing, rigging, controlled cutting, mechanical equipment, or other methods selected by Contractor according to site conditions and Contractor’s professional judgment.
  3. Remove the trunk, limbs, and reasonably collectible woody debris generated by the removal.
  4. Cut the remaining stump as low as reasonably practicable before grinding.
  5. Grind the stump to a reasonable depth below the surrounding grade, as permitted by the stump grinder, site access, underground conditions, and surrounding improvements.
  6. Grind readily accessible portions of the main stump and readily accessible surface roots immediately adjoining it.
  7. Consolidate the resulting stump grindings into the stump cavity for use as backfill.
  8. Leave the stump-grinding area reasonably level and neat following ordinary settling and consolidation.

Stump grinding does not include:

  • Complete excavation or removal of the tree’s root system;
  • Removal of every lateral or feeder root;
  • Grinding beyond the area reasonably accessible to the stump grinder;
  • Removal or disposal of the stump grindings;
  • Importing topsoil or other fill;
  • Compaction to structural or construction-grade standards;
  • Finish grading, seeding, sod installation, or landscaping;
  • Prevention or correction of future decomposition or settlement.

Customer understands that stump grindings contain organic wood material and may decompose and settle over time. Contractor is not responsible for later settlement, replenishment of fill, fungal growth associated with decomposing wood, or restoration of the area unless separately agreed in writing.

B. Dead shrub removal

  1. Remove the dead shrubs presently located along the back fence line and identified during the site inspection.
  2. Reasonably excavate or mechanically remove each shrub’s main rootball where practicable.
  3. Remove the resulting shrubs, main rootballs, and reasonably collectible debris from the property.
  4. Exercise reasonable care around the irrigation components known or observed to be near the shrubs.

The parties acknowledge that irrigation is located in or near the shrub-removal area. Contractor will use reasonable care to avoid damaging known or reasonably observable irrigation components. Because shrub roots may be intertwined with irrigation pipes, fittings, wires, or emitters, complete avoidance of incidental disturbance cannot be guaranteed.

Shrub removal does not include:

  • Removal of every root associated with the shrubs;
  • Extensive excavation of smaller feeder roots extending beyond the main rootballs;
  • Excavation beneath the fence, patio, hardscape, or neighboring property;
  • Removal of extensive roots where removal could damage irrigation, fencing, utilities, hardscape, or other improvements;
  • Irrigation redesign, rerouting, replacement, or repair unless damage is directly caused by Contractor’s failure to exercise reasonable care;
  • Importing soil, finish grading, planting, seeding, or installation of replacement landscaping;
  • Prevention of future sprouts or regrowth from roots remaining in the soil.

Contractor may leave smaller feeder roots and any root material extending materially beyond the reasonably identifiable main rootball. Contractor may also cut and leave roots in place when further excavation could reasonably threaten irrigation, fencing, utilities, hardscape, neighboring property, or other improvements.

If an irrigation line or component must be disconnected, moved, or repaired to complete the work safely, Contractor may stop the affected work and discuss the condition with Customer. Irrigation repair or modification will require a written change order unless the repair is Contractor’s responsibility under this Agreement.

C. Patio planter boxes

  1. Empty the existing planter boxes located along the patio perimeter.
  2. Remove the existing loose soil, dead vegetation, roots, weeds, mulch, and ordinary planting material from those boxes.
  3. Haul away the removed planter contents.

This work does not include demolition, movement, repair, refinishing, waterproofing, reconstruction, or replacement of the planter boxes; removal of concrete, masonry, metal reinforcement, drainage infrastructure, or permanently bonded material; or installation of new soil or plants.

If concealed masonry, reinforcement, drainage material, utility components, hazardous material, or material that cannot be removed through ordinary manual excavation is encountered, Contractor may stop the affected work and propose a written change order.

D. Cleanup

Contractor will perform a reasonable final cleanup of debris generated directly by the contracted work. Cleanup does not mean restoration of the property to a condition identical to its condition before work began, removal of unrelated preexisting debris, or removal of every particle of sawdust, wood chip, soil, or organic matter.

5. Dead-tree and site risk acknowledgment

Customer acknowledges that the oak is dead, is close to the residence, and may contain hidden or unpredictable defects, including internal decay, brittle wood, detached limbs, cracks, cavities, weak root attachment, insects, embedded metal, or structural failure not visible before work begins.

These can include falling or swinging material, equipment movement, vibration, noise, dust, sawdust, flying chips, temporary access obstruction, and incidental disturbance to lawns and soil.

Contractor will use reasonable care and methods appropriate to the observed conditions. Contractor does not guarantee that a dead or structurally compromised tree will behave like sound wood during removal.

Nothing in this Agreement releases Contractor from liability for Contractor’s gross negligence, willful misconduct, or any responsibility that cannot lawfully be waived.

  • Invisible pet fencing;
  • Private electrical lines;
  • Gas or water lines;
  • Drainage systems;
  • Septic components;
  • Internet, telephone, or cable lines;
  • Footings, buried concrete, metal, rocks, and other obstructions.

The known presence of irrigation near the shrub-removal area has been disclosed. Customer remains responsible for identifying its approximate routing and any known valves, wires, heads, fittings, or other components that are not readily visible.

Public utility markings generally do not identify owner-installed or privately maintained systems. Customer is responsible for arranging private utility locating when necessary.

Contractor is not responsible for damage to an underground or concealed item that was not accurately disclosed, visibly apparent, or properly marked before work began, unless the damage resulted from Contractor’s failure to exercise reasonable care.

If concealed concrete, metal, utilities, excessive stone, hazardous material, unstable structures, unusually extensive roots, or other materially different conditions are encountered, Contractor may stop the affected work. The parties may then agree upon a change order, an alternative method, or deletion of the affected portion with an equitable price adjustment.
