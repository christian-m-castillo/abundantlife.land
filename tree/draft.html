Tree Trimming & Pruning

Good Pruning Has a Purpose.

Sometimes the right solution is to remove a dead limb. Sometimes it's to improve clearance, reduce the loading on a particular branch, or correct a developing structural problem.

Proper pruning begins by identifying an objective. The location, size, and number of cuts should serve that objective while respecting the tree's biology and structural integrity.

Our pruning approach is informed by the ANSI A300 tree care standards, including appropriate pruning objectives, cut placement, and avoidance of harmful practices such as indiscriminate topping.

  • Dead and broken branch removal
  • Structural and corrective pruning
  • Selective crown reduction
  • Roof and building clearance

Professional Practices

The Difference Between Cutting Trees and Caring for Them

Professional tree work involves more than owning a chainsaw. Sound recommendations, thoughtful work planning, and safe execution are essential.

We use recognized arboricultural standards as a framework for planning and performing tree work, including the ANSI A300 tree care standards and ANSI Z133 safety standard.

ANSI A300 — Tree Care Practices

ANSI A300 provides professional guidance for tree care operations, including pruning. It helps establish what work is intended to accomplish and how that work should be specified.

For homeowners, this means a pruning recommendation should be more specific than simply "trim the tree."

We consider the pruning objective, tree condition, branch structure, appropriate cuts, and the amount of foliage being removed.

ANSI Z133 — Arboricultural Safety

ANSI Z133 addresses safety requirements for arboricultural operations, including climbing, chainsaw use, rigging, electrical hazards, and worksite practices.

Our work planning considers the hazards of the tree, the surrounding environment, equipment selection, communication, and the people working on site.

We use job-specific hazard assessments and recognize stop-work authority when conditions require reassessment.

Our approach: Make recommendations based on the actual tree and site, specify the intended work clearly, and plan the operation with safety in mind.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions About Tree Service?

How much does tree removal cost?

Pricing depends on tree size, condition, access, proximity to structures, equipment requirements, and debris handling. We provide a job-specific estimate rather than relying on a generic per-tree price.

Can you remove a tree close to my house?

Many trees near structures can be removed using appropriate climbing, sectional dismantling, and rigging techniques. The method depends on the tree's condition and the specific site. Some jobs may require additional equipment or precautions.

Do you trim branches away from roofs?

Yes. We provide roof, building, driveway, and walkway clearance pruning. We consider both the clearance needed and the effect of pruning on the remaining tree.

Will pruning hurt my tree?

Pruning creates wounds and removes living tissue when live branches are cut. Appropriate pruning can provide important benefits, but excessive or poorly placed cuts can cause harm. We aim to limit pruning to a clearly defined objective and use appropriate techniques.

Do you follow ANSI tree care standards?

Our tree-care recommendations and work planning are informed by the ANSI A300 tree care standards and ANSI Z133 arboricultural safety standard. Specific work requirements are established through the job scope and site assessment.

Are you insured?

Yes. Abundant Life Land & Tree LLC carries insurance for its tree service operations. Please contact us if you need documentation relevant to your project.

Do you offer free estimates?

We provide estimates for proposed tree work. More detailed consultations, tree assessments, and written reports may involve a separate fee, which we'll explain before proceeding.

What if I'm not sure whether a tree is dangerous?

Contact us to describe what you've observed. Visible decay, cracks, hanging branches, root disturbance, and changes in tree condition may warrant assessment. Avoid entering an area beneath an unstable or hanging limb. If a tree is contacting electrical conductors, stay clear and contact the utility provider.
